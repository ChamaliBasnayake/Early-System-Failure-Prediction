# Phase 5 — optimisation study: what the evidence says (Question 3.b)

Source: `results/optimisation_study.md` (tables and figures), `results/curves/*.json`, `experiments/log.csv` (phase 5 rows, human notes applied by `src/utils/apply_annotations.py`). All runs N = 5 min, seed 11, validation only. Reference v1 = Adam 1e-3, batch 128, one 64-unit LSTM per channel, dropout 0.3, LayerNorm, last-state pooling, BCE with pos_weight = √ratio. **Yardstick: the three-seed spread of v1 itself is 0.0305 ± 0.0115 PR-AUC (0.0226–0.0437), so no single-seed change smaller than ≈ 0.012 can be called an effect.** Twenty-two configurations were evaluated against the 25 random-search configurations of the XGBoost baseline: the same order of tuning effort.

## F5.1 Learning rate (4 runs): the default sits at the low edge of the usable range
1e-4 and 3e-4 stopped at best epoch 1 (PR-AUC 0.021 / 0.020, ROC 0.55): with the CPU budget (patience 5) the model never left initialisation — under-training, not over-regularisation (training loss still falling). 3e-3 (0.028, ROC 0.676) is within noise. 1e-2 was *expected to diverge* and did not (0.0225, ROC 0.673): Adam's per-parameter step normalisation and LayerNorm kept the gradients bounded. Conclusion: for this model the learning rate matters through *how many useful epochs fit in the budget*, not through stability.

## F5.2 Batch size (2 runs): not a lever
32 → 0.0266, 512 → 0.0294, both within noise; batch 32 took 2.2× longer per epoch, batch 512 needed more epochs (best at 14) so total time rose to 4446 s. Gradient noise neither helps nor hurts a 1 %-prevalence ranking problem; the number of positive examples per epoch is what limits learning, and that is fixed by the data.

## F5.3 Depth and width (3 runs): capacity is not the bottleneck
Two LSTM layers (137 k params) → 0.0432, identical to v1 seed 11 (0.0437) at 1.9× the parameters and 1.8× the time. Hidden 32 (33 k) → 0.0301, hidden 128 (196 k, 6045 s) → 0.0381, both within noise; hidden 128 shows the only over-fitting signature in the study (lowest training loss, highest validation loss 0.3308). A 60-minute window of collapsed messages has little hierarchical structure for depth to exploit and only ~300 positive incidents to learn from, so the smallest configuration on the plateau (1 × 64) was kept. This is the direct answer to "is the network deep enough": depth was tested and did not pay.

## F5.4 Pooling and normalisation (2 runs): order information is secondary
Mean+max pooling over time (0.0411) equals last-state pooling within noise: an order-insensitive summary loses nothing, consistent with Phase 3's finding that *which* messages appear matters more than their sequence. Removing LayerNorm (0.0288) is also within noise because the inputs are already log-scaled; LayerNorm was retained because it is what made lr 1e-2 stable (F5.1).

## F5.5 Regularisation and class weighting (4 runs)
Dropout 0.1 → 0.0255 (ROC 0.678, earliest validation-loss minimum = mild over-fitting of the head of the ranking); dropout 0.5 → 0.0379 with the best recall@1 % FAR of the study (0.073): heavier dropout trades bulk ranking (ROC 0.642) for the top of the ranking. pos_weight = 1 (0.0277) and pos_weight = full ratio ≈ 65 (0.0326) bracket v1 within noise; the full ratio was *expected to over-fire* (saturated scores) and did not — scores shift upward but the ranking is unchanged. Class weighting therefore affects where the threshold lands, not the ranking; the √ratio compromise was kept for calibration of the FAR thresholds.

## F5.6 Optimisers (3 runs): why Adam, with evidence
- **SGD + momentum, lr 0.01**: 0.0218, best epoch 20 of 25 and still improving. Without per-parameter adaptive steps, the rarely-updated embedding rows (most templates appear in few windows) and the LSTM gate biases move slowly; it would need several times the epoch budget.
- **SGD + momentum, lr 0.1**: the **best ROC-AUC of the whole study (0.694)** but PR-AUC 0.0217 and **recall at 1 % FAR = 0**. Large uniform steps learned the bulk ordering well yet flattened the score distribution so that no positive ever reached the top 1 %. This single run is the strongest argument for PR-AUC/recall-at-FAR as the primary metric: a model can have the best ROC and be useless at the operating point.
- **AdamW, weight decay 1e-2**: 0.0305, indistinguishable from Adam. Decoupled weight decay adds nothing when dropout already regularises a 71 k-parameter model.
Mechanism: Adam's second-moment normalisation equalises step sizes across parameters with very different gradient frequencies (frequent LSTM weights vs rare embedding rows); that is precisely the structure of this problem, and it is why Adam converges in 10–15 epochs where SGD does not converge in 25.

## F5.7 The lecturer-approved fourth hyper-parameter: local window length (2 runs) — unsuccessful, and informative
6 h → 0.0205 (best epoch 2), 24 h → 0.0210 (best epoch 1). Longer unit history adds no precursor information (the failing midplane is silent before most failures) and dilutes the last hour's lines; the runs stopped almost immediately because there was nothing more to learn than v1 already had. The same study on the tabular model (Phase 3c) showed no effect either. The signal is in the last hour and in the machine-wide channel.

## F5.8 Architectures (2 runs): CNN unsuccessful, semantic LSTM selected on mechanism
The 1-D CNN (kernels 3/5/7 × 64, masked max-pool; 102 k params) is the worst run: 0.0234, ROC 0.601, still improving at epoch 20. Local n-gram patterns over collapsed items carry less than the LSTM's whole-window state, and max-pooling discards the repeat-count and Δt context the LSTM integrates. The semantic LSTM (frozen MiniLM text embeddings projected to 64-d) scored 0.0327 — within noise of v1 — and was chosen as the final model **before the test on mechanism**: it can represent messages never seen in training (61 % of test lines). Its three-seed spread then turned out to be 0.0004 against 0.0115 for the template LSTM: the frozen embedding removed the initialisation variance of the learned embedding, which is the most practically useful result of the study.

## Unsuccessful attempts, diagnosed (≥ 2 required; 8 recorded as `unsuccessful` in the log)
lr 1e-4 and 3e-4 (under-trained within budget), SGD 0.01 (too slow without adaptive steps), SGD 0.1 (good ROC, zero recall at FAR — flattened scores), 6 h and 24 h windows (no information beyond the last hour), the CNN (local patterns < whole-window state), and the collapsed N = 15 seed-11 final run (stopped at epoch 1 at 1.4× prevalence while seeds 22/33 trained normally — reported as mean ± sd, not dropped). Two pre-registered expectations were wrong and are recorded: lr 1e-2 did not diverge; pos_weight = ratio did not over-fire.
